# Factor-sweep ("performance surface") findings

Script: `run_factor_sweep.py`. Raw results: `experiments/factor_sweep_results.csv`
(1500 rows, 0 errors, full run in 59s). Full grid tables: `experiments/factor_sweep_summary.log`.
Regime calibration check: `experiments/factor_sweep_calibration.log`. Heatmap grid:
`experiments/factor_sweep_surface.png`.

## What was run

Reviewer's ask: independently vary structural signal, text signal, image signal,
cross-modal agreement, and missingness; compare structure-only, content-only,
simple concatenation, spectral+content, fixed-alpha NF-MCD, and adaptive-alpha
NF-MCD; report a performance surface over cross-modal agreement x missingness.

- **Graphs**: `n_nodes=320`, `n_communities=4`, synthetic SBM + community-correlated
  text/image embeddings, generated by a **local** extension of
  `nf_mcd.datasets.generate_synthetic_multimodal_graph` (`run_factor_sweep.generate_factor_graph`)
  that adds independently-controllable `text_centroid_scale` / `image_centroid_scale`
  (the shipped generator ties both modalities to one shared `centroid_scale`). This
  local function is not wired into `nf_mcd/datasets.py` — see the script's module
  docstring for why (a second agent was working in `nf_mcd/` and other `run_*.py`
  files in the same non-git-worktree directory at the same time; only new files
  were touched).
- **Two representative regimes** (structural signal x content signal, the reviewer's
  items 1-3, held fixed while sweeping the two primary axes):
  - `strong_struct_weak_content`: `p_in=0.22, p_out=0.02` (well-separated SBM blocks),
    `text_scale=image_scale=0.35` (weak, near-noise centroid separation, `noise_scale=1.0`).
  - `weak_struct_strong_content`: `p_in=0.055, p_out=0.045` (SBM blocks barely denser than
    random), `text_scale=image_scale=4.0` (strongly separated centroids).
  - **Calibration check** (agreement=0.9, missingness=0.0, 5 seeds, `experiments/factor_sweep_calibration.log`):
    `strong_struct_weak_content` gives structure-only ONMI=0.986±0.01 vs. content-only
    ONMI=0.000±0.00; `weak_struct_strong_content` inverts this to structure-only
    ONMI=0.003±0.00 vs. content-only ONMI=0.829±0.02. The two regimes are a clean,
    verified asymmetry, not just nominally-different parameters.
- **Two primary "surface" axes**, full grid (not reduced — the full 5x5x2 grid at
  5 seeds/cell ran in under a minute, so no coarsening was needed):
  - Cross-modal agreement in `{0.9, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1}` (`= 1 - misalignment_rate`;
    a "misaligned" node's image embedding is drawn from a different community's
    centroid, i.e. cross-modal similarity no better than chance).
  - Missingness in `{0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8}` (`missing_modality_rate`, MCAR, matches
    the generator's existing definition used throughout this project).
- **5 seeds per cell** (`0-4`) — above the reviewer's stated 3-seed minimum, at
  negligible extra cost.
- **Six methods**, `run_factor_sweep.run_method`: `structure_only` = `nf_mcd.baselines.nfmcd_structure_only`
  (NF-MCD's own structure-only ablation, reused verbatim); `content_only` =
  `nf_mcd.baselines.fcm_content` (reused verbatim); `simple_concat` = a new local
  function (`run_factor_sweep.simple_concat`): structural embedding + raw L2-normalised
  content concatenated with **no** fusion mechanism (no CCA, no ANFIS confidence, no
  alpha weighting), fuzzy c-means (m=1.5) on the result; `spectral_content` =
  `nf_mcd.baselines.spectral_graph_content` (this project's strongest baseline
  throughout, reused verbatim); `nfmcd_alpha_fixed` = `nf_mcd.baselines.nfmcd_alpha_fixed`
  (alpha_min=alpha_max=0.5, reused verbatim); `nfmcd_adaptive` = `nf_mcd.baselines.nfmcd_full`
  (plain `NFMCD` defaults, reused verbatim). Metrics (`nf_mcd.baselines.score`, reused
  verbatim, identical to every other experiment in this repo): LFK overlapping NMI
  (primary) and modularity.

Total: 2 regimes x 5 agreement levels x 5 missingness levels x 5 seeds x 6 methods
= 1500 runs, 0 errors.

## Headline numbers

**Overall mean ONMI per method, averaged over all 25 (agreement, missingness)
cells in each regime:**

| method | strong_struct_weak_content | weak_struct_strong_content |
|---|---:|---:|
| structure_only | 0.986 | 0.003 |
| content_only | 0.000 | 0.498 |
| simple_concat | 0.108 | 0.141 |
| spectral_content | **0.993** | **0.528** |
| nfmcd_alpha_fixed | 0.965 | 0.005 |
| nfmcd_adaptive | 0.900 | 0.003 |

**Cell-by-cell winner counts (25 cells/regime):**
- `strong_struct_weak_content`: spectral_content wins 20/25 cells, structure_only
  wins the remaining 5 (the high-agreement/high-missingness corner, where content is
  so degraded that even spectral+content's small kNN-content contribution slightly
  hurts and plain structure edges it out).
- `weak_struct_strong_content`: spectral_content wins 20/25 cells, content_only wins
  5/25 (the zero-missingness column at high/mid agreement, where raw content alone
  is enough and spectral's extra graph term adds noise from the near-random structure).
- **Neither fixed-alpha nor adaptive-alpha NF-MCD ever wins a single cell in either
  regime.** Spectral+content (the existing "strongest baseline" per the manuscript)
  wins 40/50 cells outright.

## Central claim test: does fusion earn its complexity, and when does it hurt?

**Regime 1 (strong structure, weak content) — fusion is mostly harmless but adds
no value, with one exception.** Both NF-MCD variants track close to the
structure-only ceiling (fixed: 0.965, adaptive: 0.900, vs. structure-only 0.986),
i.e. the fuzzy alpha mechanism correctly down-weights the near-useless content most
of the time. But **adaptive-alpha NF-MCD collapsed to ONMI < 0.05 in 5/125 individual
runs (4%) at this regime's most adversarial corner — low cross-modal agreement
(0.5/0.3/0.1) combined with zero missingness** (e.g. agreement=0.5, missing=0.0:
mean ONMI = 0.454 ± 0.37 over 5 seeds — 2 of 5 seeds collapsed, 3 did not).
**Fixed-alpha (0.5) never collapsed in this regime (0/125 runs).** Diagnosed
directly (`U.max(axis=1).mean()`, the project's standard collapse diagnostic from
`experiments/collapsep08_summary.log`): the collapsed adaptive runs hit
`mean_umax = 0.2500`, exactly `1/k` for `k=4` — the same fuzzy-c-means membership
collapse documented elsewhere in this repo, here triggered specifically by adaptive
alpha (which, in this regime, computed mean alpha ≈ 0.57-0.61, i.e. *more* weight on
the near-noise content than fixed alpha=0.5 gives) rather than by the (unrelated)
`m` or `structural_dim` settings that earlier diagnoses focused on. **This is a
concrete instance of adaptive fusion actively hurting relative to the simpler
fixed-alpha ablation**, worth flagging in the manuscript's fusion-mechanism
discussion — the ANFIS confidence estimate is not discriminating well between
truly-aligned and truly-misaligned pairs when the underlying content signal itself
is this weak (a mechanism the calibration experiment below probes directly).

**Regime 2 (weak structure, strong content) — fusion catastrophically hurts.**
Structure alone is useless here (ONMI=0.003, as intended), and content alone
recovers real signal (ONMI up to 0.829 at agreement=0.9/missingness=0). **Both
fixed-alpha and adaptive-alpha NF-MCD collapse to near-zero ONMI (0.000-0.014)
across every single one of the 25 agreement x missingness cells** — confirmed via
the same `mean_umax` diagnostic (`0.2500` exactly, every seed checked). The
content signal that `content_only` and `spectral_content` successfully use is
present in NF-MCD's fused features too (it goes through the same CCA projection),
but injecting the 4-dimensional structural block from an almost-random graph — even
down-weighted by `alpha_min=0.15` in the worst case — is enough to push fuzzy
c-means (`m=1.5`) into the collapsed regime for every node, destroying the hard
partition regardless of what alpha does. **This directly confirms the paper's own
prior collapse diagnosis (`experiments/collapsep08_summary.log`) generalizes beyond
its original k=32/high-missingness setting: a weak-but-nonzero noisy structural
block is sufficient to trigger it even at k=4 with abundant, informative content and
zero missingness.** Simple concatenation also fails here (mean ONMI 0.141, and drops
to exactly 0.000 once missingness ≥ 0.6 or agreement is low) — for a different,
non-fuzzy-collapse reason: `simple_concat`'s raw un-fused content block (896 raw
dims: 384 text + 512 image, L2-normalised) numerically dominates the 4-dimensional
structural block in Euclidean fuzzy-c-means distance regardless of which block is
actually informative, so it cannot exploit structure even where structure would
help and is dragged down by noise-scale content once enough of it goes missing.

## What this means for the manuscript's central claim

The reviewer's question was "when does multimodal fusion help or hurt". This
sweep gives a direct, numbers-backed answer for the current default
configuration:
1. **It essentially never *helps* relative to the existing spectral+content
   baseline** in either tested regime — spectral+content matches or beats both
   NF-MCD variants in every cell but 5 (all 5 in the near-content-free corner of
   regime 1, where structure_only itself equals spectral+content within noise).
2. **It can *hurt* severely** — not gracefully degrading toward the
   better-informed signal, but triggering full fuzzy c-means collapse
   (`mean_umax = 1/k`) once structure is weak enough, independent of how good the
   content signal is or how much of it is missing.
3. Within that collapse regime, **fixed alpha=0.5 is more robust than the
   adaptive default** in the borderline case (regime 1's low-agreement corner):
   0/125 vs. 4% collapsed runs. The adaptive mechanism's extra complexity is not
   only failing to earn its keep here, it is measurably less stable than the
   naive ablation it is meant to improve on.

## Honest limitations / what was simplified

- Two regimes, not a full independent 4-D sweep over structural x text x image
  signal strength (the reviewer explicitly allowed this: "at minimum: one
  strong-structure/weak-content and one weak-structure/strong-content setting").
  Text and image signal strength were kept equal to each other within each
  regime (both varied together, `text_scale == image_scale`); a genuinely
  independent text-vs-image asymmetry (e.g. strong text + weak image at fixed
  structure) was not run — the local generator supports it (separate
  `text_centroid_scale`/`image_centroid_scale` parameters), it just wasn't a
  third axis in this grid, to keep the two primary axes (agreement, missingness)
  as the full 5x5 surface the reviewer asked for.
- `k=4` and `n=320` throughout (matches this project's typical small-graph
  synthetic settings elsewhere, e.g. `run_overlap3_diagnosis.py`'s `n=300`);
  the collapse mechanism identified here was previously documented only at
  `k=32`/`n=8000` (`run_collapse_p08_diagnosis.py`) — this sweep is new evidence
  it also occurs at much smaller k/n, not a re-run of that larger-scale result.
- The `simple_concat` baseline is new code written for this experiment (not
  present in `nf_mcd/baselines.py`); it deliberately has no scale-balancing
  between blocks (that's the point — it's the "no fusion mechanism" reference
  point), so its poor performance should be read as "naive concatenation fails
  as expected," not as a bug.
- `overlap_rate=0` throughout (hard ground-truth partitions only); the sweep
  does not touch overlapping-community behavior.
- No deeper root-cause analysis of *why* adaptive alpha's mean alpha (~0.57-0.61)
  comes out higher than the fixed 0.5 in regime 1's collapsing corner despite low
  true cross-modal agreement there — flagged as a concrete, reproducible
  observation (seeds/cells given above), not chased further; a natural follow-up
  is the calibration behavior of confidence itself under weak content, which
  Experiment 2 addresses in a different (real + synthetic mismatch-injection)
  setting.

## Reproducibility

`py run_factor_sweep.py calibrate` (regime sanity check) → `py run_factor_sweep.py run --workers 4`
(resumable: finished `(regime, agreement, missingness, seed, method)` rows are
skipped on rerun, appended to CSV with flush+fsync per job) → `py run_factor_sweep.py summary`
(rebuilds `factor_sweep_summary.log` and `factor_sweep_surface.png` from the CSV).
Confirmed: ran to completion, 1500/1500 jobs, 0 errors, ~59s wall time with 4 workers.
